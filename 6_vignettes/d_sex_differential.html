Scenario

Figure 2(d). Genotype G and sex affect the outcome Y. Participation S depends on G with different effects in men and women, on sex, and on Y in opposite directions in men and women (following Lee et al. 2022). Pooling men and women then induces associations between participation SNPs and sex, and through sex with Y.

SNPs affect Y (Y_snps), participation (P_snps), or neither (null). Two scenarios differ only in how common participation is:

  • d_low: 5% of the population participates;
  • d_high: 50% participates.

Both have about 15,000 participants.

Participation SNPs, which have no effect on Y, become associated with it, and Y-acting SNPs are biased when participation is rare.

2. Diagnostic: participation GWAS

There is no participation indicator for non-participants here. Instead, sex-differential participation is detected by testing SNP-sex associations among participants. Autosomal SNPs cannot cause biological sex, so any association indicates selection.

for (sc in scen) {
  d  <- dat[[sc]]
  sg <- sex_gwas(d$pheno$Sex, d$G)
  s  <- summarise_participation(sg)
  cat(sprintf("\n%s: lambda = %.2f\n", sc, attr(s, "lambda")))
  print(s, row.names = FALSE)
  cat("Proportion of SNPs with p < 0.05, by group:\n")
  print(round(sapply(d$groups, function(i) mean(sg$p[i] < 0.05)), 2))
}

Both participation SNPs and Y-acting SNPs are associated with sex, because participation depends on the genotype and on the outcome differently in men and women. The diagnostic detects either route.

3. Correction: sex-stratified GWAS meta-analysis

GWAS are run separately in men and women and combined by inverse-variance weighted fixed-effects meta-analysis (Lee et al. 2022). A pooled GWAS adjusted for sex is shown for comparison.

fits <- setNames(lapply(scen, function(sc) {
  p <- dat[[sc]]$pheno
  list(adj   = run_gwas(p$Y, dat[[sc]]$G, covar = p[, "Sex", drop = FALSE]),
       strat = sex_stratified_gwas(p$Y, dat[[sc]]$G, p$Sex))
}), scen)

res <- do.call(rbind, lapply(scen, function(sc) rbind(
  by_scen(pooled[[sc]],      sc, "pooled"),
  by_scen(fits[[sc]]$adj,    sc, "pooled, sex-adjusted"),
  by_scen(fits[[sc]]$strat,  sc, "sex-stratified meta")
)))

Stratification removes the bias when participation is rare (d_low) but not when it is common (d_high). There, participation within each sex still depends on both the participation SNPs and Y, so conditioning on it induces an association between them. Stratification and sex adjustment perform equally here; stratification also allows sex-specific SNP effects, which Cochran’s Q tests.

4. Summary

plot_df <- do.call(rbind, lapply(scen, function(sc) {
  d   <- dat[[sc]]
  grp <- rep(names(d$groups), lengths(d$groups))[order(unlist(d$groups))]
  rbind(
    data.frame(scenario = sc, analysis = "pooled",              snp_group = grp,
               truth = d$truth, beta = pooled[[sc]]$beta),
    data.frame(scenario = sc, analysis = "sex-stratified meta", snp_group = grp,
               truth = d$truth, beta = fits[[sc]]$strat$beta)
  )
}))
plot_df$scenario <- factor(plot_df$scenario, levels = scen)

ggplot(plot_df, aes(truth, beta, colour = snp_group)) +
  geom_abline(slope = 1, intercept = 0, linetype = "dashed", colour = "grey50") +
  geom_point(alpha = 0.7) +
  facet_grid(scenario ~ analysis) +
  labs(x = "True SNP effect", y = "Estimated SNP effect",
       colour = "SNP group") +
  theme_bw()

Points on the dashed line are unbiased. When participation is rare, pooling men and women pulls participation SNPs away from zero and distorts Y-acting SNPs, and stratification restores them. When participation is common, some participation SNPs remain off the line after stratification.
