Scenario

Figure 2(a). Genotype G affects the outcome Y directly and through a measured, heritable predictor Z (e.g. education). An unmeasured factor U also affects Y. Participation S depends on:

  • a1: Z and E (E predicts participation but not Y), i.e. selection on measured factors;
  • a2: Z, E and Y itself, i.e. selection on the outcome.

N is a negative control outcome: it has no genetic effects but shares non-genetic causes with Z and Y. SNPs are grouped by what they affect: Y directly (Y_snps), Z (Z_snps), or nothing (null).

Selection on Z biases the SNPs that act through Z (a1). Selection on the outcome also biases the SNPs acting directly on Y (a2). Null SNPs are barely affected, so a genome-wide summary such as λ hides the problem.

3. Corrections

Inverse probability weighting

ipw_res <- list()
for (sc in c("S_a1", "S_a2")) {
  sel <- p[[sc]] == 1
  w   <- ipw_weights(p[[sc]], covar = p[, c("Z", "E")])
  cat(sprintf("%s: %d participants, effective sample size %.0f\n",
              sc, sum(sel), attr(w, "ess")))
  ipw_res[[sc]] <- ipw_gwas(p$Y[sel], d$G[sel, ], w)
}

IPW removes bias from selection on measured factors (a1) at the cost of a much smaller effective sample size. It cannot correct selection on the outcome (a2).

Heckman correction

Heckman estimates direct SNP effects conditional on Z, so it is compared with truth_direct. When SNPs affect selection (a2), the SNP must be in the selection equation.

sel <- p$S_a2 == 1
naive_adjZ <- run_gwas(p$Y[sel], d$G[sel, ], covar = p[sel, "Z", drop = FALSE])
hk_once <- heckman_gwas(p$S_a2, p$Y, d$G, sel_covar = p[, c("Z", "E")],
                        out_covar = p[, "Z", drop = FALSE], per_snp = FALSE)
hk_snp  <- heckman_gwas(p$S_a2, p$Y, d$G, sel_covar = p[, c("Z", "E")],
                        out_covar = p[, "Z", drop = FALSE], per_snp = TRUE)

4. Summary

grp <- rep(names(d$groups), lengths(d$groups))[order(unlist(d$groups))]

est <- function(res, truth, label) {
  data.frame(analysis = label, snp_group = grp, truth = truth,
             beta = res$beta)
}

plot_df <- rbind(
  est(a1,           d$truth,        "a1: naive"),
  est(ipw_res$S_a1, d$truth,        "a1: IPW"),
  est(a2,           d$truth,        "a2: naive"),
  est(hk_snp,       d$truth_direct, "a2: Heckman (SNP in selection)")
)
plot_df$analysis <- factor(plot_df$analysis, levels = unique(plot_df$analysis))

ggplot(plot_df, aes(truth, beta, colour = snp_group)) +
  geom_abline(slope = 1, intercept = 0, linetype = "dashed", colour = "grey50") +
  geom_point(alpha = 0.7) +
  facet_wrap(~ analysis, nrow = 2) +
  labs(x = "True SNP effect", y = "Estimated SNP effect",
       colour = "SNP group") +
  theme_bw()

Points on the dashed line are unbiased. Under selection on measured factors (a1), IPW restores the Z-acting SNPs to the line. Under selection on the outcome (a2), Heckman with the SNP in the selection equation recovers the direct effects, provided an exclusion variable (E) is available.
